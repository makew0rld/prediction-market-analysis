Market calibration

Each resolved binary market's probability is sampled at checkpoints and scored against how it resolved. A perfectly calibrated market sits on the diagonal: of markets at 60%, 60% resolve YES.

Calibration

Checkpoints are either a fraction of each market's lifetime, or a fixed number of days after creation. Lifetime checkpoints are distorted by markets that resolve YES as soon as their event happens: their checkpoints land just before the event. Day checkpoints avoid this, but only include markets still open on that day, so later days cover fewer, longer markets.

Markets are grouped into bins centred on round probabilities (the 60% bin holds markets at 55–65%). Each dot sits at the bin's average market probability. The bars show how far a dot could land from the true rate by luck alone (Wilson 95% intervals). Marker area scales with the number of markets. Click legend entries to hide checkpoints.

Accuracy over a market's lifetime

Brier: the mean of (probability − outcome)², where the outcome is 1 for YES and 0 for NO. Lower is better; always guessing 50% scores 0.25. Lifetime checkpoints score the same markets every time, so their Brier scores are directly comparable. Day checkpoints drop markets that have already closed, so later days are a different, smaller set of markets.
